The problem

Research

The work wasn't slow. Finding it was.

I did not start with interviews. Ask a salesperson about their process and you get the pitch. So I sat in on real deals and logged every time someone left one tool to find something in another.

Deal shadowing · 6 advisors, 9 live dealsInterviews · 4 sales engineersInventory · every screen state in production

Lost context, not lost time

Advisors are fast at the job. What slowed them down was rebuilding the picture every time they came back. Which quote did the client see. Who did I rule out, and why.

The argument, not the answer

I assumed a matching engine was the prize. It was not. "The system picked these three" makes an advisor replaceable. They needed a reason they could say in their own words.

Waiting is most of the deal

Most of a deal's life is spent pending. The old product only had screens for moments when something happened. Everything in between was invisible.

Design

Three shapes for the work.

I built all three. The one that looked best in review lost to the one that worked on a live client call.

A. Hard wizard

One step at a time, forward only. Real deals loop back. A client changes scope in week three and the wizard has no answer.

Rejected

B. Hub of centers

Everything one click from a home page. Better than the launcher, but still a menu. It shows what exists, not where this deal stands.

Rejected

C. Open sequence, with a rail

Order without enforcement. Files travel with the deal. The cost: every step has to make sense when entered out of order.

Chosen

Logos, or rows.

Comparing providers is the moment the advisor is sharing their screen with the client. That decided it.

A wall of logos

Everyone liked it in review, including me. In use, advisors picked the logos they already knew. It rewarded recognition, not fit.

Change 1

The score wins the row.

Every column had the same weight, so the fit score was one more cell and nobody looked at it. It got its own column and its own type. It is out of five on purpose. A percentage invites "why 82 and not 85".

Change 2

Registration is a checkbox.

As its own page with its own button, "Register" felt like signing something, and advisors stalled. But the decision was already made in step 2. So the control moved to where the decision happens.

Change 3

Waiting, made visible.

An assessment now has a status page: what was sent, when, by whom, and what has come back so far. Nobody has to ask "did you get this".

What I would change · 1

I measured late, and measured the wrong thing first.

We counted visits to each state. Research had already told me the waiting was the product. I should have measured how long deals sat in a state from day one.

What I would change · 2

I designed one side of the handoff.

Advisors got a clear view of a pending assessment. The sales engineers doing it got nothing new. Every advisor's wait is a sales engineer's backlog. Next time, both ends ship together.
